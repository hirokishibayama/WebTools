# Editorial Philosophy

Everyday Japan explains ordinary Japanese life to foreigners.

## Quality Gates (OS)

Before human review, every article passes:

1. **Introduction Agent** — surprise, visitor question, reading benefit
2. **Japanese Perspective Agent** — local everyday experience without invented first-person memoir
3. **Editorial Quality Check** — Cultural Accuracy / Japanese Perspective / Reader Engagement / SEO

Japanese readers should think: **"Yes, that's true."**

---

## Four Conditions

Every article must satisfy all four:

### 1. Local perspective

Japanese people should read it and think: **"Yes, that's true."**

Write from how locals actually experience daily life — not from a tourist checklist.

### 2. Explain WHY, not only WHAT

Always include historical, cultural, or social reasons.

Surface description without explanation fails the editorial standard.

### 3. Never use a "Japan is amazing" tone

Avoid:

- exaggeration
- nationalism
- stereotypes
- clickbait wonder

Be factual and balanced.

### 4. Help first-time visitors understand daily life

Assume the reader has never lived in Japan.

Primary audience:

- English-speaking travelers
- people planning to visit Japan
- people interested in deeper cultural understanding

## Tone of Voice

- Native, natural English
- Magazine feature, not travel guide
- Calm, clear, specific
- Short paragraphs and concrete scenes
- Japanese terms introduced with brief explanation (e.g. **konbini (コンビニ)**)

## Article Structure (required)

1. Hook — realistic scene
2. The Question — what feels different?
3. Local Perspective — how Japanese people see it
4. Cultural Background — history, society, lifestyle
5. Real-life Examples
6. Visitor Tips
7. FAQ
8. Closing Insight

## Anti-Patterns

Do **not** write:

- generic travel blogs
- top-10 tourist attractions
- AI-generated listicles
- "Japan is amazing" clickbait
- Wikipedia summaries
- surface-level cultural explanations

The goal is not to impress readers.

The goal is to help them understand why everyday life in Japan works the way it does.

## Reference Standard

The article in `reference/` defines writing style, structure, tone, detail level, SEO balance, and editorial philosophy.

All future articles should feel like they were written by the same editor.
