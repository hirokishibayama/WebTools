## Facts

- Some people in Japan bow during telephone calls even though the other party cannot see them. — supported. Widely observed in service and office settings; not universal.
- The other party can sense the bow through the phone. — unsupported. Article rejects telepathy/mystical framing.
- The movement is related to in-person ojigi and keigo, amplified by service training. — interpretation-flagged. Presented as interpretation of embodied politeness + scripts.
- Younger people and casual calls do it less. — supported. Register/role variation; not a census statistic.
- All Japanese people bow on every call. — false-stereotype. Article lists exceptions: friends, trains, casual shops.

## Interesting Observations

- Everyday local practice is flatter than tourist photography.
- Exceptions by building, age, region, and setting are part of the explanation, not footnotes only.

## Possible FAQ

### Q: Can the other person tell if I bow?

A: Not on a normal voice call. People in the room can see you.

### Q: Is this because phones are sacred?

A: No. Habit plus service training.

### Q: Do all Japanese people do this?

A: No. Formal and customer-facing calls more than casual ones.
