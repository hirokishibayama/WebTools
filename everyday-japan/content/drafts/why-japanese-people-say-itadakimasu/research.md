## Facts

- Itadakimasu is a common pre-meal phrase meaning humble receiving. — supported. Standard Japanese usage.
- It is exactly a religious prayer for most speakers. — false-stereotype. Article treats daily use as manners/thanks; religious overlap possible but not required belief.
- School lunch often includes a group itadakimasu. — supported. Common elementary-school practice; wording/cues vary by school.
- Gochisousama is a common after-meal closer. — supported. Standard pairing.
- Visitors are required to say it. — false-stereotype. Article: not required; appreciated if matching the table.
- Everyone in Japan says it at every snack. — false-stereotype. Snacks, izakaya, solo eating often skip or mumble.

## Interesting Observations

## Possible FAQ

### Q: Is itadakimasu a prayer?

A: For most daily speakers, not a doctrinal prayer. It can look prayer-like.

### Q: Do I have to say it in restaurants?

A: No.

### Q: What should I say after eating?

A: Gochisousama deshita is the common closer toward a host.
