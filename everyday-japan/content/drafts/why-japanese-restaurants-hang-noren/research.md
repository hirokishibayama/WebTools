## Facts

- A noren is a split shop curtain used at many restaurants and some bathhouses. — supported. Standard Japanese commercial/sento practice.
- Hanging/taking in the noren often signals open/closed for small shops. — supported-hedged. Custom, not statute; lights and hours still matter.
- Every Japanese restaurant uses a noren. — false-stereotype. Malls, chains, cafes often do not.
- Noren are uniquely Japanese and never existed elsewhere. — unsupported. Article notes door curtains in other cultures; local form is distinctive.
- Noren-wake is a business metaphor for sharing a shop name/lineage. — supported. Standard Japanese business vocabulary; not a visitor ritual.

## Possible FAQ

### Q: Does a hanging noren always mean open?

A: Often for small shops that use it that way; not a law.

### Q: Do all restaurants have one?

A: No.

### Q: Is it only for restaurants?

A: No. Sento and some other shops use them too.
