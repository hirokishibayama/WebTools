## Facts

- Kairo are disposable chemical warmers commonly sold in Japanese winter retail. — supported. Konbini/drugstore seasonal merchandising.
- Stick-on vs pocket types exist. — supported. Haru vs haranai product split.
- Many homes heat room-by-room rather than whole-house central air as a universal default. — supported-hedged. Older stock and AC/kerosene/kotatsu patterns; new insulation varies.
- Everyone in Japan uses kairo daily in winter. — false-stereotype. Region, job, and preference vary; mild prefectures use less.
- Exact kairo market size in yen. — hedged-omitted. No unsourced market figure.
- Misuse can cause burns. — supported. Package warnings; consumer-product risk.

## Possible FAQ

### Q: What is a kairo?

A: A chemical warmer, usually disposable; adhesive and pocket types.

### Q: Do Japanese homes not have heating?

A: They do, often by room. Gaps remain in halls, waits, and commutes.
