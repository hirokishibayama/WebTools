## Facts

- Dryers and bathroom dryers exist in Japan; balcony hanging is still common. — supported. Appliance shops sell combination machines; many apartments have yokushitsu kansoki. Ownership shares vary; article hedges percentages.
- Every Japanese building allows balcony laundry. — false-stereotype. Some condo bylaws restrict visible laundry or futons on outer rails.
- Futons are commonly aired because they hold moisture and fit poorly in many consumer dryers. — supported. Lived housing practice; coin laundry used as backup.
- Cedar pollen season can push drying indoors for allergy-sensitive households. — supported. Common public-health/lifestyle advice; not universal protocol.
- Laundry can freeze on poles in cold regions. — supported. Ordinary winter observation in snow country / inland cold.
- Exact national dryer-ownership or electricity-cost figures. — hedged-omitted. No unsourced market size or household percentage claimed.

## Possible FAQ

### Q: Do Japanese homes not have dryers?

A: Many have some drying method; daily heat-drying of every load is less of a default in many households. Shares vary.

### Q: Is hanging laundry required?

A: No. Common where space, weather, and rules allow.

### Q: Do all apartments allow balcony laundry?

A: No. Check house rules.
